How to Buy a Used Camera on eBay: 9 Checks Before You Pay

Used cameras can offer excellent value, but the listing photos rarely tell the whole story. Use this checklist to compare specific listings before buying.

1. Check the exact model and included items

Confirm the model number and whether the listing includes a lens, charger, battery, caps, straps and original accessories. A body-only listing may be cheaper but cost more once you add a lens.

2. Ask about shutter count

For cameras with mechanical shutters, shutter count can help indicate use. It is not a guarantee of remaining life; compare it with the model's typical durability and the camera's overall condition.

3. Inspect the sensor and sample images

Look for dust, scratches, dead pixels and uneven exposure. Request an unedited sample photo if the listing does not show image quality.

4. Examine lens glass and autofocus

Check for fungus, haze, scratches and sticky aperture blades. Confirm autofocus and stabilization work if the lens supports them.

5. Check ports, screen and controls

Review photos of the battery compartment, memory-card slot, hot shoe, buttons and display. Damage to hinges or ports can be costly to repair.

6. Understand battery health

Older batteries may hold less charge. Budget for a compatible replacement and verify whether the charger is included.

7. Compare the seller and return terms

Read recent seller feedback, the item condition description, shipping terms and the actual return policy. Avoid assuming that all used listings have the same protections.

8. Calculate the full price

Include shipping, taxes, batteries, memory cards and any lens you still need. Compare the total with refurbished alternatives and new entry-level models.

9. Check compatibility before checkout

Confirm lens mount, battery type, card format, video features and software support for your intended use.
