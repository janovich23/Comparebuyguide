How to Check Battery Health Before Buying a Used Laptop

A used laptop may look great in photos while its battery is near the end of its useful life. Ask for battery information before paying, especially if you plan to use the computer away from an outlet.

For Windows laptops: request a battery report

Ask the seller to run Windows Command Prompt and enter powercfg /batteryreport. Windows saves an HTML report; the seller can share a screenshot showing the design capacity and full charge capacity. The latter is the amount the battery can currently hold according to the system. A lower full charge capacity relative to design capacity indicates wear, though measurements are estimates.

For MacBooks: check battery condition and cycle count

Ask for a screenshot of Battery Health in macOS settings and, where available, the cycle count from System Information → Power. Apple battery service recommendations and cycle limits vary by model, so confirm the exact model and consult Apple's guidance rather than treating one cycle count as universally good or bad.

Questions to ask the seller

  • What are the full charge capacity and design capacity, or the reported battery condition?
  • Is the battery original or has it been replaced?
  • Does the laptop charge normally, and is the charger included?
  • Does the listing offer returns if the reported battery condition is inaccurate?
  • Can the seller share a recent screenshot from the actual device?

Factor replacement into the price

Compare the delivered purchase price plus any expected battery service cost with a better-condition or refurbished alternative. Replacement difficulty and price vary widely by model; some batteries are not designed for simple user replacement. Check the manufacturer's service options before deciding a worn battery is a bargain.

Red flags

Be cautious when a seller cannot provide basic battery information, shows screenshots from a different model, reports charging problems, or lists a swollen battery. Do not use or charge a device with a visibly swollen or damaged battery; seek qualified service.

Bottom line

Battery health is one part of a good used-laptop deal. Also verify processor, RAM, storage, screen condition, account locks, warranty, and returns. Use our nine-point used laptop buying checklist before you buy.
